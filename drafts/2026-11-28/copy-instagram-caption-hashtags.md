You found a recipe, a list of book recommendations or a perfect set of hashtags in an Instagram caption, and now you want the text. In the Instagram app, long-pressing a caption usually does nothing, so there is no obvious way to copy it. The good news is that every phone and computer has a built-in workaround. Here are the methods that work, from quickest to most reliable.

## Why Instagram captions are hard to copy

The Instagram app shows captions as part of the post layout rather than as normal selectable text. In most versions, you cannot highlight a caption with a long press the way you can in a web page or a messaging app. Comments behave similarly. This is a design choice in the app, not a problem with your phone.

The website is different. When you view a post on instagram.com in a browser, the caption is ordinary text you can select and copy. That is the key to the first method.

## Three ways to copy a caption

Pick the method that suits the device in your hand. All three are built in, so there is nothing to install.

### Method 1: open the post in a browser

This works on any device and copies the exact text, including emoji and hashtags.

1. In the Instagram app, tap the paper-plane **Share** icon under the post and choose **Copy link**.
2. Open your browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox or any other) and paste the link into the address bar.
3. If Instagram asks you to open the app, choose to stay in the browser instead. You may need to log in to see the full caption.
4. Tap **more** under the caption if it is cut off.
5. Long-press a word in the caption, then drag the selection handles to cover the text you need.
6. Tap **Copy**.

On a computer, open the post on instagram.com, click and drag across the caption, then press Ctrl+C (Windows) or Cmd+C (Mac).

> Tip: on a phone, switching the browser to desktop mode (in the browser menu) sometimes makes the caption easier to select, because the text is shown in a wider column.

### Method 2: Live Text on iPhone

Newer iPhones can read text straight from an image, which is handy when you are already in the app.

1. Open the post and tap **more** so the whole caption is visible on screen.
2. Take a screenshot (Side button and Volume Up together).
3. Tap the screenshot thumbnail in the corner to open it.
4. Long-press on the caption text in the screenshot. It becomes selectable, just like normal text.
5. Adjust the selection and tap **Copy**.

You can also tap the Live Text icon (a small square with lines) at the bottom of the screenshot to highlight all detected text at once. Delete the screenshot afterwards if you do not need it.

### Method 3: Google Lens or Circle to Search on Android

Android phones have similar tools, depending on the model.

1. Open the post with the full caption visible.
2. On phones that support **Circle to Search**, long-press the home button or navigation bar, then circle or tap the caption and choose the option to select text.
3. On other phones, take a screenshot, open it in Google Photos, and tap **Lens**. Choose **Text**, select the caption, and tap **Copy**.

Text recognition from a screenshot is very accurate for clear captions but can stumble on unusual fonts in emoji-heavy text. Always read the copied text once before you paste it somewhere important.

## Copying hashtags only

Hashtags are often at the end of the caption, or posted in the first comment to keep the caption tidy.

- **In the caption:** use any method above and select only the block of hashtags.
- **In a comment:** on a computer, open the post on instagram.com and select the comment text the same way you would a caption. On a phone, the screenshot methods work for comments too.

If you plan to reuse hashtags on your own posts, pick the ones that actually describe your content. Copying a long list that does not match your post rarely helps it reach the right people.

## What does not work, and what to avoid

A few things are worth knowing so you do not waste time:

- **Private accounts:** if the post is from a private account you do not follow, you cannot see the caption, and no tool can show it to you.
- **Apps that ask for your login:** some caption or hashtag apps ask you to sign in with your Instagram account. That gives a third party access to your account, which is a real security risk. None of the methods above need it, and IGtoDownload never asks for your Instagram password.
- **Deleted posts:** once a post is removed, the caption goes with it. If a caption matters to you, copy it when you see it.

### Credit where it is due

Captions are writing, and they belong to the person who wrote them. Copying a recipe or a quote for your own reference is normal. If you repost someone's caption, or use it in your own content, mention the original account. It is the respectful thing to do and it helps creators get found.
