Instagram lets you watch as many videos as you like, but it gives you no button to keep someone else's video on your phone. On Android the fix is simple: copy the link, fetch the original file, and let your gallery pick it up. This guide walks through the whole process, including where the file lands and how to make it a one-tap job next time.

## What you need before you start

You do not need a special app from the Play Store, a rooted phone or an Instagram password. You need three things:

- The Instagram app (or instagram.com open in Chrome)
- A browser such as Chrome, Samsung Internet or Firefox
- A public video, reel or post that contains a video

The last point matters. Videos from private accounts cannot be downloaded by any honest tool, because Instagram only shows them to approved followers. If a tool asks you to log in with your Instagram account to get around that, close it. IGtoDownload never asks for your password and does not store the media you download.

## Step-by-step: save a video to your phone

1. Open the video or reel in the Instagram app.
2. Tap the paper-plane **Share** icon under the video.
3. Scroll the row of options and tap **Copy link**. A small "Link copied" message confirms it.
4. Open Chrome and go to the [video downloader](/video). For reels, the [reels downloader](/reels) works the same way.
5. Tap the input box and paste the link. The **Paste** button fills it in one tap if your browser allows it.
6. Wait a moment while the video is found, then check the quality and file size shown in the result.
7. Tap the download button. Chrome shows a download notification at the top of the screen when the MP4 is saved.

That is all. The file is the original MP4 Instagram serves, so it keeps the uploaded resolution and sound, with no watermark added on top.

> Tip: if the post is a carousel that mixes photos and videos, every slide appears in the result. You can save just the video, or use the download-all option to grab every slide at once.

## Where the video ends up on Android

Downloaded files go to the **Download** folder in your phone's internal storage. File names start with `igtodownload.com_`, which makes them easy to spot among everything else you have downloaded.

How quickly it shows up in your gallery depends on the app you use:

- **Samsung Gallery** usually lists it within a minute under the Albums tab, in an album called Download.
- **Google Photos** shows it under **Collections** (or **Library** on older versions) in the **On this device** section, inside the Download folder. If you want it backed up, open that folder and turn on backup for it.
- **Files by Google** or **My Files** always shows it instantly under Downloads, even when the gallery has not refreshed yet.

If the video does not appear at all, open Chrome, tap the three-dot menu and choose **Downloads**. If it is listed there, it was saved; your gallery simply has not scanned the folder yet. Restarting the gallery app usually fixes it.

### Moving it to a different album

Gallery apps let you move or copy files between albums. In Samsung Gallery, long-press the video, tap **More**, then **Move** or **Copy** and pick an album. In Google Photos, you can add it to an album from the three-dot menu. The original stays in Download unless you move it.

## Common problems and quick fixes

**"The link doesn't work."** Make sure you copied the post link, not a profile link. A video link contains `/reel/` or `/p/` followed by a short code. Profile links only contain a username.

**"It says the post is private or unavailable."** The account may be private, the post may have been deleted, or it may be restricted in your country. None of these can be bypassed.

**"The video saved but has no sound."** Check your media volume, not just the ringer volume. Press a volume key while a video is playing so the slider controls media. If the reel uses a song that Instagram has muted, it will be silent on Instagram too.

**"The download stops halfway."** Long videos are bigger files. Switch from a weak mobile signal to Wi-Fi, keep Chrome open until it finishes, and check that you have free storage space.

## Use downloaded videos responsibly

Saving a video for yourself, to watch offline or keep a recipe or tutorial, is the most common reason people download. Re-uploading someone else's work as your own is a different matter. If you share a downloaded video, credit the creator and ask permission when you plan to use it publicly or commercially.
