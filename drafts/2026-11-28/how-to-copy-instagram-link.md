Almost everything you want to do with an Instagram post outside the app starts with its link: sending it to a friend, saving it in your notes, or downloading it. Instagram puts the **Copy link** option in a slightly different place for each type of content, which is why so many people get stuck. Here is where to find it for every format, on phone and computer.

## Copying links in the Instagram app

Start with the type of content you have open. The steps below follow the current Instagram app on both iPhone and Android; button positions can shift slightly between versions, but the names stay the same.

### Reels

Reels have the most visible share button.

1. Open the reel in the Instagram app.
2. Tap the paper-plane **Share** icon on the right side of the screen (or under the video in the feed).
3. In the panel that slides up, scroll the bottom row of options and tap **Copy link**.

You will see a short confirmation that the link was copied. A reel link looks like `instagram.com/reel/` followed by a short code.

> Tip: links copied from the app often end with a tracking part such as `?igsh=...`. You can leave it there; it does not stop the link from working. Deleting everything after the question mark gives you a cleaner link to share.

### Photo and video posts, including carousels

Feed posts work almost the same way.

1. Find the post in your feed or on the account's profile.
2. Tap the paper-plane icon under the post, then **Copy link**.
3. Alternatively, tap the three dots at the top right of the post. **Copy link** is usually in that menu too.

Post links look like `instagram.com/p/` plus a code. A carousel has a single link for the whole post, not one per slide. When you paste that link into the [photo downloader](/photo), every slide appears, so you can pick individual images or download them all.

### Stories

Stories are where most people get lost, because the paper-plane icon on a story sends it as a direct message rather than showing a share menu.

1. Open the story.
2. Tap the three dots in the top right corner.
3. Choose **Copy link**. On some versions you may need to tap **Share** first and then **Copy link**.

If the option is missing, the account is probably private. Instagram only offers story links for public accounts, and the story will disappear 24 hours after it was posted unless the owner adds it to a highlight. A story link looks like `instagram.com/stories/username/` followed by a long number. You can paste it into the [story downloader](/story) while the story is still live.

### Highlights

Highlights are saved stories pinned to a profile, and they stay available until the owner removes them.

1. Open the account's profile and tap the highlight circle.
2. While it plays, tap the three dots in the corner.
3. Tap **Copy link** (some versions call it **Copy highlight link**).

Highlight links usually contain `/stories/highlights/`. Our guide on [downloading Instagram stories and highlights](/blog/download-instagram-stories-highlights) explains what you can do with them next.

### Profiles

Sometimes you want the account itself rather than one post.

1. Open the profile.
2. Tap the three dots at the top right.
3. Choose **Copy profile URL** or **Share this profile** and then copy.

A profile link is just `instagram.com/username`. Keep in mind that a profile link is not a post link. Downloaders need a link to a specific reel, post or story, so pasting a profile link will not fetch any media.

## Copying links on a computer

On instagram.com in a desktop browser, the address bar does most of the work.

1. Click a post, reel or story to open it.
2. The address bar updates to the item's own link. Click it, select all, and copy (Ctrl+C on Windows, Cmd+C on Mac).
3. You can also click the paper-plane icon and choose **Copy link**, which gives the same result.

One catch: when you open a post from someone's profile grid, it may appear in a pop-up window on top of the profile. The address bar still changes to the post link, so copy it while the pop-up is open, not after closing it.

## How to check that you copied the right link

A quick look at the link tells you what it points to:

- `/reel/CODE` — a reel
- `/p/CODE` — a feed post (photo, video or carousel)
- `/stories/username/NUMBER` — a single story
- `/stories/highlights/NUMBER` — a highlight
- `/username` only — a profile, not a post

If you paste a link and nothing happens, compare it against this list first. A surprising number of failed downloads come from a profile link or a link that was cut off when it was copied from a message.

## A note on private accounts

You can copy the link of a private post if you follow the account, but that link only works for people who are approved followers. Downloaders cannot open it, and you should be wary of any service that claims it can. Respecting that boundary is part of using Instagram content fairly.
